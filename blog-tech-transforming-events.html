Building the Future of Event Management: What the Indian Market is Actually Screaming at Us

Published on June 2026 • Utsanova Technologies • 6–7 min read

Introduction

Where there is a will, there is a way; and where there is a wedding in India, there is a will, a way, fourteen vendors, and utter disorganization.

Every wedding, college fest, corporate conference, product launch, trade expo, and music concert happening in this country right now is being held together by duct tape and blind faith.

The caterer is triple-booked and is yet to receive that memo. The decorator is wrapping a mehendi setup in Jaipur while negotiating a corporate gig in Pune on the other ear.

The event planner is currently playing a high-stakes game of digital whack-a-mole, powered entirely by a spreadsheet that has abandoned all earthly hope and dark under-eye circles so severe they are now legally a part of their identity.

The glue holding this absolute disaster together? Forwarded PDFs and pure delusion.

It's a spectacular mess, which is exactly why it's a massive goldmine just sitting there in plain sight. Totally ignored, and begging for someone to actually fix it.

Fortunately, some of us aren't asleep at the wheel. Utsanova to the rescue.

So... How Big Is This Mess, Exactly?

India's event management industry is not a niche side gig. It is not some emerging little sector quietly finding its feet. It is a full-scale, economy-grade, genuinely enormous market that people are still somehow treating like a hobby business.

The India Events Market hit USD 15.39 billion in 2025. It is projected to grow at 7.60% every single year through 2035, potentially reaching USD 32.02 billion. Now that's definitely not a rounding error.

And then there is the software layer sitting on top of all of this physical bedlam.

The event management software market in India generated USD 634.5 million in 2025 and is on track to reach USD 2,167.4 million by 2033, growing at a CAGR of 16.2% per year.

Sixteen. Point. Two. Percent. Compounding. For seven years. That is not a market waking up slowly.

Allow me to set the cat among the pigeons: the hidden truth.

North India holds the dominant revenue share of 28.2% of this entire market. Which means if you are a platform based in Rajasthan, building specifically for India, you are sitting in the literal geographic centre of the largest slice of this pie.

Just putting that out there for absolutely no reason whatsoever.

The Wedding Industry: 87% Unorganized and That Is a Problem

If the events market broadly is large, the wedding industry specifically is staggering in a way that physically stops people mid-conversation.

The Indian wedding industry injects ₹6–6.5 lakh crore into the economy during the 45-day peak season alone.

  • The average couple hires 14 distinct vendors for their wedding.
  • The industry provides 5–10 million full-time jobs and another 10–20 million seasonal jobs.

Fourteen vendors. Per wedding. Coordinated primarily through phone calls, aunty referrals, and the occasional spreadsheet that someone's cousin built and swore was "basically professional."

Now here is the part that should make your eye twitch slightly: 87% of this industry is unorganized.

Which sounds like a problem. And it is. But it is also the specific category of problem that a well-built platform can walk directly into and solve in a way that creates enormous value for everyone involved.

Like the whole "I do not have experience for the job, but I never had the job to gain experience" paradox, except the platform is the experience.

We are getting somewhere.

The India wedding services market reached USD 32.98 billion in 2025. It is projected to reach USD 92.67 billion by 2034.

And the most important detail hiding in those numbers: online bookings are currently the minority, but they are set to grow at the fastest CAGR of 18.3% through 2034.

Event planning services themselves are projected to grow at 17.95% CAGR.

The market isn't waiting around; it's aggressively digitizing under everyone's noses.

That golden window of opportunity isn't propped open with a brick; it's actively slamming shut. And it'll probably be completely locked by the time some legacy board of directors finishes debating whether they should even build the platform in the first place.

What's Actually Keeping Organizers Awake at 3 AM? (Hint: It's Not Passion.)

Let's stop talking about opportunity for one moment and actually talk about what goes wrong. Because opportunity is only useful if you understand what you are solving.

Problem One: The "Absolute Mayhem" Approach

Planning a massive event means juggling venues, transport, vendors, and legal rules. It sounds fancy, but in reality, all of these things love to crash into each other and explode into a spectacular misadventure.

This is just a polite way of saying: managing vendors without one single system is a full-time nightmare that nobody actually signed up for.

Instead of doing real work, your life becomes a circus of hundreds of frantic phone calls and forwarded quotes. Then, two weeks later, a vendor disputes your deposit, and those quotes are completely gone.

Suddenly, the left hand has no idea what the right hand is doing, and you are trapped in absolute hullabaloo.

We have all been that desperate person, sweating and scrolling through WhatsApp like crazy to find a text from three weeks ago.

Now, imagine that missing text contains the entire budget for a 1500-person wedding. Your heart drops. Your stomach turns. It is what makes you want to evaporate into thin air.

Problem Two: Trust and Transparency Are Missing

One startup's stated goal is to standardize vendor services and bring transparency to pricing in a USD 130 billion unorganized ecosystem.

Read that back slowly. 130 billion dollars. Unorganized.

Neither standardized pricing nor consistent quality benchmarks exist. There is no way for someone in Jaipur to know whether the photographer they're booking is actually as good as the portfolio suggests, or whether that portfolio belongs to someone who shot one event in 2019 and has been recycling those same three photos ever since.

Problem Three: The Offline-to-Online Transition Is Happening… But Slowly

66.8% of wedding bookings still happen offline. As in, in person, face to face.

Which means if you build an app and just expect people to suddenly stop doing that, you are going to have a very quiet app and a very confused expression on your face.

People are not avoiding technology because they are dumb. They are avoiding it because booking a ₹10 lakh wedding vendor through an unknown app feels like a gamble nobody signed up for.

The trust is just not there yet. And trust, unfortunately, cannot be forced.

The platform that actually wins isn't the one hoping for a stroke of luck with a shiny app, but the one that puts its nose to the grindstone to build digital trust, brick by brick.

Problem Four: Data Lives Nowhere

Post-event, most organizers have essentially zero usable data.

Who attended, how much was spent, which vendors delivered, and what the guest experience actually looked like—none of it is captured systematically.

Every event starts from scratch. Every vendor relationship is rebuilt from absolute cipher; and this is coming from a country that saves 47 versions of "Sharma Ji Caterer Jio" and still cannot find the right one.

It is unfair. Why must the industry endure this melancholy?

What Technology Is Actually Changing (And What It Has Not Figured Out Yet)

Increasing use of virtual and hybrid events, increased automation in event planning, and more people owning smartphones or having access to the Internet are currently redefining the event management software market in India.

The industry is evolving. But the important question is where it is heading.

Which Technology Is Thriving?

Ticketing. Entry through QR code. Payment gateways. Event discovery basics.

This is now a bare minimum. Any competent platform has this covered.

Good. Great. Next.

Which Technology Needs Work?

Vendor ecosystem. End-to-end integration. CRM systems developed specifically for event planners.

One software that does everything—from a vendor being alerted of an inquiry to an automatic report being created after an event when the planner is already enjoying dinner.

Here is the institutional support that startups in this ecosystem are missing out on: the "Meet in India" government initiative, the establishment of new venues such as Bharat Mandapam and Jio World Convention Centre, and an increasing trend of companies moving to experiential marketing have all been strengthening the market from above.

There is more government spending on physical infrastructure for events now than there was before. Corporate India is increasingly allocating budgets for experiential marketing.

The scenario is quite positive compared to what it was five years back.

This is akin to the road getting ready while everyone is debating what kind of a car to take.

Hop on. Drive.

The Vendor Ecosystem: The Massive Unsolved Problem

Here is the part of the market that is genuinely underserved and genuinely massive.

With over 10 million weddings taking place each year across India, the wedding sector generates more economic activity than the GDP of entire countries. Yet the industry remains largely fragmented and analog, creating unprecedented opportunities for digitization.

Think about what "fragmented and analog" actually means from a vendor's perspective.

A caterer in Jaipur who is exceptional at their job has essentially no way of reaching a customer on the other side of the city unless someone refers them.

Their reputation lives entirely in word of mouth. Their pricing has neither a benchmark nor does their availability have any digital record.

They are functionally invisible to anyone discovering vendors through platforms rather than personal networks.

It's a classic chicken-and-egg mess: platforms need vendors to matter, and vendors need platforms to be seen, but nobody in India has actually solved this equation at scale yet.

The fix isn't rocket science; it's just smart.

Mobile-first technology that handles the matching and planning for you, especially in tier-2 and tier-3 cities, where the real growth is happening while everyone else is still fighting over metros.

The biggest opportunity isn't in the metros anymore.

It's in cities like Jaipur, Indore, Lucknow, Coimbatore, and Nagpur, where growing demand is finally catching up with digital infrastructure that still has plenty of room to evolve.

So, Why Does This Market Actually Need a Unified Ecosystem?

Here is the core thesis, and I'm only going to say it once: India's event industry does not need another isolated app.

It doesn't need a shinier ticketing platform, a prettier vendor directory, or a separate CRM to add to the digital pile.

Those tools are fine. They are completely missing the point.

The goal is one platform where everything actually talks to each other.

Insane concept, I know.

The Indian events market was valued at roughly USD 47.75 billion in 2025 and is projected to skyrocket to nearly USD 119.77 billion by 2034.

Because India cannot even have an identity crisis quietly, it has to do it at scale.

Corporate events are leading the growth, obviously, because nothing says "we have money" like a conference with a theme and a photo wall.

International organizers are increasingly viewing India as a cost-competitive gateway into South Asia, which is reassuring because the last time foreign companies arrived to "do business," things got... historically complicated.

The competition is no longer domestic.

The actual clients didn't wait around for the industry to catch up, either.

Couples are already booking vendors on their phones, tracking budgets digitally, and livestreaming weddings for relatives who claimed they "couldn't make it."

The window is open. For now.

The Indian event industry is at an inflection point.

The market is massive enough to support serious tech, and this digital wave is moving so fast that standing still isn't a safe bet; it's just a slow-motion forfeit.

The canyon between the tools organizers currently use and what they actually need is so wide that whichever platform fixes it is going to own the entire space.

Conclusion

The future of event management in India is unified, digital, and vendor-connected.

We don't need a crystal ball to see it coming; it's already kicking us in the face through the numbers, the client behavior, and a 16.64% CAGR rate that is bellowing for everyone to wake up.

Utsanova has built it. The market is ready. The only thing still pending is you.

So either get on board, or enjoy explaining to your clients why their venue booking is still pending in the big twenty-six.

There is a problem. There is a solution.
